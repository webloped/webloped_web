Recognising abuse

Abuse can be physical, sexual, emotional, financial or digital. It is about power and control, not about what you did or didn’t do.

Common warning signs
  • Controlling who you see, where you go, or what you wear
  • Constant criticism, humiliation, or threats
  • Monitoring your phone, messages, or location
  • Controlling money or stopping you from working
  • Isolating you from friends and family
  • Any physical harm, or threats of harm

If any of this sounds familiar, it can help to talk it through — see crisis support.

Healthy relationships

In a healthy relationship you feel safe saying no, disagreements happen without fear, your boundaries and friendships are respected, and support goes both ways.

What this looks like day to day
  • Respect for your decisions, including about money and your body
  • Honest conversation, even when it is difficult
  • Space for your friendships and family
  • Apologies that come with changed behaviour

Rights and routes to legal support

In Ontario, you can call 911 in an emergency without anyone’s permission. Legal protections can include restraining orders and peace bonds, and free legal help may be available depending on your situation.

Where to start
  • Barbra Schlifer Commemorative Clinic — legal representation and counselling for women who have experienced violence (416-323-9149)
  • Legal Aid Ontario — funds lawyers for eligible clients (416-979-1446)
  • Victim Services Toronto — practical help after crime or sudden tragedy (416-808-7066)

This is general information, not legal advice. Verify details directly with each service.

Digital privacy and safer browsing

If someone monitors your devices, a few habits can reduce what is visible — but no technique makes activity invisible on a monitored device or network.

Practical steps
  • Use a device the person does not monitor when you can — a friend’s phone, a work computer, a public library
  • Private or incognito windows don’t hide activity from someone monitoring the device or network
  • Only clear call logs or browsing history if it is safe to do so — sudden gaps can raise suspicion
  • Quick Exit (top right, or the Esc key) opens Google in this tab right away, but it does not delete your browser history

Privacy

Precisely what this demo does — and doesn’t do — with your activity.

On this page

  • This demo sends nothing anywhere. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no trackers.
  • Anything you type into the safety plan or search stays in this page’s memory only. It disappears if you leave or reload.
  • Checklist ticks are not saved anywhere.

Quick Exit

Quick Exit opens Google in this tab right away. It does not delete your browser history, and it cannot hide your activity from someone monitoring this device or network.

Discreet mode

Discreet mode hides this page behind a weather screen. It does not hide your browsing history or make your activity invisible on this device.

Beyond this page

Your browser, your device, and your network provider may still record that you visited this site. If someone monitors your devices, consider using a safer device — a friend’s phone, a work computer, or a public library. See safer browsing.

Accessibility

This demo aims to be usable by everyone.

  • Full keyboard support: every action works with Tab, Enter and Escape.
  • Visible focus indicators on all interactive elements.
  • Decorative motion is reduced or removed when your device requests reduced motion.
  • Text and control colour contrast targets WCAG 2.2 AA.
  • Touch targets are at least 44×44 pixels.
  • Screen-reader announcements for view changes and search results.

This demo concept has no published contact channel. If you hit a barrier using it, the verified services in Find Help are the right place to turn.
