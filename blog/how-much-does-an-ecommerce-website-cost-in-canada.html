How much does an ecommerce website cost in Canada in 2026?

An online store costs more than a brochure website, and the gap is not the design. Here is what a Canadian ecommerce build really costs, what it costs every month after launch, and where the surprises hide.

So, how much does an ecommerce website cost in Canada? For a small Canadian business, a professionally built online store usually lands somewhere between $5,000 and $25,000 CAD for the build, plus $100 to $500 a month to run it. A do-it-yourself store can cost under $1,000 to set up, and a large custom store with integrations can run past $60,000. The honest answer is that the price depends on how many products you sell, how your shipping and taxes work, and who builds it.

This guide breaks down every piece of that cost in Canadian dollars: the build, the platform, the payment fees, and the monthly running costs that most quotes leave out. All figures are typical ranges published by Canadian agencies and platforms in 2026, not quotes, and every price here is before tax unless it says otherwise.

The short answer: ecommerce website costs in Canada

Most Canadian small business stores fall into one of these four buckets. Find the one that matches your situation and you have a realistic budget range.

  • DIY on Shopify or Squarespace: $0 to $1,500 to set up, plus $49 to $132 a month for the platform. You build it yourself from a template and pay with your own time. Best for testing an idea with a small catalogue.
  • Freelancer or small studio Shopify store: $5,000 to $15,000 for the build. Custom design on a proven platform, products loaded, payments and shipping configured, basic SEO in place. This is where most Canadian small businesses land.
  • Studio or agency custom store: $15,000 to $40,000 for the build. Custom design and development, often with a custom theme, integrations with accounting or inventory software, and professional copy and photography.
  • Large or complex custom store: $40,000 to $60,000 and up. Thousands of products, wholesale pricing, subscriptions, custom checkout, or integrations with an ERP or warehouse system.

The build price is only half the story. An online store also has monthly costs that a brochure website does not: platform fees, card processing on every sale, and usually a few paid apps. Budget for both before you commit.

Why an online store costs more than a regular website

A brochure website shows information. An online store moves money, and that changes everything behind the scenes. A store needs a product catalogue, a cart, a checkout, payment processing, tax calculation for every province, shipping rules, order emails, inventory tracking, and a way to handle returns. Each of those is a system that has to be set up, tested, and maintained.

The parts that add the most cost are the ones shoppers never see. Charging the right tax in Ontario, Alberta, and Quebec is three different rules, not one. Shipping a parcel from Toronto to Vancouver costs far more than shipping it across town, and the store has to quote that correctly at checkout or you eat the difference. A store that sells ten products is a different project from a store that sells five hundred, because every product needs a page, photos, a description, variants, and a price.

What actually drives the build cost

When you compare quotes, these are the levers that move the number up or down.

  • Number of products. Ten products with clean data is a weekend of setup. Five hundred products with variants, sizes, and colours is weeks of data work, and it is usually the biggest single cost driver. Clean product data saves more money than any discount.
  • Design: template or custom. A polished template with your branding is fast and affordable. A fully custom design costs more but gives you a store that does not look like every other Shopify store. Most small businesses are well served by a customized premium theme.
  • Payments. Accepting Visa and Mastercard through Shopify Payments or Stripe is standard. Adding Interac, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and buy-now-pay-later options takes extra setup, and each one changes the fee picture slightly.
  • Shipping rules. Flat-rate shipping is simple. Real-time carrier rates from Canada Post or UPS, free-shipping thresholds, local pickup, and delivery zones all add setup time. If you ship across Canada, get this right before launch, because wrong shipping quotes are the fastest way to lose money on every order.
  • Tax setup. Canada has GST, HST, PST, QST, and RST depending on the province, and the rates differ. Shopify now calculates Canadian taxes automatically, but someone still has to confirm the settings match where you are registered. Get this wrong and you either overcharge customers or under-collect tax you owe.
  • Integrations. Connecting the store to your accounting software, email marketing, inventory system, or a marketplace like Amazon or Etsy each adds cost. One integration is routine; five is a project of its own.
  • Copywriting and photography. Product photos sell the product, and a phone photo on a kitchen table does not. Budget for proper product photography and for someone to write descriptions that actually describe the product. This is the part owners most often skip and most often regret skipping.
  • Migration. Moving from an old platform, or from selling on Etsy or Instagram, means moving products, customers, and order history without breaking links or losing search rankings. Our guide to migrating a website without losing SEO covers what to protect.

Shopify costs in Canada: the platform most small stores choose

Shopify is the default choice for Canadian small business stores, and it bills in Canadian dollars, which removes the exchange-rate surprise that comes with US-priced platforms. As published on Shopify's Canadian pricing page in late 2026, the plans are:

  • Basic: $49 a month billed monthly, or $37 a month billed yearly. Online card rate through Shopify Payments is 2.8% plus 30 cents per order.
  • Grow: $132 a month billed monthly, or $99 a month billed yearly. Online card rate is 2.6% plus 30 cents.
  • Advanced: $517 a month billed monthly, or $389 a month billed yearly. Online card rate is 2.4% plus 30 cents.

There is also a Starter plan at about $7 a month for selling through links and social media without a full storefront, and Shopify Plus for large businesses starting around $3,400 a month. Prices change, so check the current pricing page before you budget, and read our full breakdown of how much Shopify costs in Canada for the worked monthly totals.

Two things catch new store owners out. First, the plan fee is the smallest part of the bill once you are selling: on the Basic plan, a store doing $10,000 a month in online sales pays roughly $310 in card fees on top of the $49 plan fee. Second, if you use a payment provider other than Shopify Payments, Shopify adds an extra transaction fee of 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, and 0.6% on Advanced, on top of what that provider charges. For most Canadian stores, Shopify Payments is the cheaper route.

Shopify vs WooCommerce vs custom: which route costs less?

The platform you pick changes both the build cost and the monthly cost, and there is no single cheapest answer. It depends on what you sell and who will run the store day to day.

Shopify is the simplest route for most Canadian small businesses. Hosting, security, and updates are included, the checkout is proven, and Canadian tax and shipping tools are built in. The trade-off is the monthly fee and the per-order card fees, which grow as you sell more.

WooCommerce on WordPress has no monthly platform fee, which looks cheaper at first. But you pay for hosting, security, backups, and updates yourself, and the store needs more ongoing maintenance. It suits businesses that already run WordPress and have someone technical looking after the site, or stores with unusual product rules that Shopify handles poorly.

A fully custom store is the most expensive to build and the most expensive to maintain, and it only makes sense when no platform fits: very large catalogues, wholesale pricing rules, subscriptions with unusual billing, or deep integration with a warehouse or ERP system. If a platform can do the job, a platform will almost always cost less over three years.

The monthly running costs after launch

A realistic monthly budget for a small Canadian store on Shopify looks like this:

  • Platform fee: $37 to $132 a month, depending on the plan and whether you pay yearly.
  • Card processing: 2.4% to 2.8% plus 30 cents per online order through Shopify Payments. This scales with sales, so it is your biggest variable cost.
  • Domain name: roughly $15 to $25 a year for a .ca or .com, registered in your own name.
  • Apps: $0 to $200 a month. Most stores need one or two paid apps, for email marketing, reviews, or shipping labels. Audit these every few months, because unused apps are the most common wasted spend we see.
  • Theme: a one-time cost of roughly $100 to $400 CAD for a premium theme, if you do not use a free one.
  • Maintenance and updates: either your own time or a monthly care plan. See our guide to website maintenance costs in Canada for what that typically runs.

Add it up and a small store doing modest sales typically spends $100 to $300 a month before card fees, and card fees add roughly 3% of revenue on top. A store doing $5,000 a month in sales should expect around $150 in card fees plus the platform and app costs. None of this includes advertising, which is a separate budget and the part most new store owners underestimate.

Canadian details that catch store owners out

Selling online in Canada has a few quirks that US pricing guides never mention.

Tax is provincial, not national. A customer in Alberta pays 5% GST. A customer in Ontario pays 13% HST. Quebec adds QST on top of GST, and British Columbia and Saskatchewan have their own PST. Your store has to charge the right combination for the customer's province, and you need to be registered to collect in the provinces where you have a tax obligation. Shopify Tax now automates much of this, but confirm the settings with your accountant before launch.

Shipping across Canada is expensive. Canada is large and sparsely populated, and a parcel to a rural address can cost three times what the same parcel costs within the GTA. Set shipping zones and rates deliberately, consider a free-shipping threshold that protects your margin, and test checkout with addresses in a few provinces before you launch.

Interac matters. Many Canadian shoppers prefer Interac, and offering it at checkout can lift conversion, especially for lower-priced products. It is available through Shopify Payments and through some other Canadian gateways.

French matters in Quebec. If you sell into Quebec, French product descriptions and checkout text are not optional extras. They are expected, and in some cases legally required for consumer-facing material. Budget for translation if Quebec is a real market for you.

Prices are in CAD, but your costs might not be. If you buy inventory in US dollars and sell in Canadian dollars, the exchange rate is part of your margin. Price with a buffer, and re-check it when the dollar moves.

Hidden costs and common mistakes

The quotes that look cheapest usually leave something out. Watch for these.

  • Product data cleanup. If your product list lives in spreadsheets, photos, and memory, someone has to turn it into clean, consistent data before it can go into a store. Ask who does that work and what it costs.
  • Content and photography. A store with no photos does not sell. Budget for photography before launch, not after.
  • Payment holds and payouts. New Shopify Payments accounts can have payout holds while the account is verified. Have your business documents ready, and keep a cash buffer for the first month.
  • SEO and launch marketing. A store with no visitors makes no sales. Launch without a plan for getting traffic, whether that is Google, social media, email, or local search, and the store sits empty. Our guide to increasing website traffic covers the free channels first.
  • Apps that pile up. Every app is a monthly fee and a potential point of failure. Start with the fewest apps you need and add one at a time, with a reason each time.

How to budget and get an accurate quote

Before you ask anyone for a price, write down five things: how many products you sell, whether you need custom design or a template will do, how you ship and where, which payment methods you want, and whether you have product photos and descriptions ready. That one page of notes is the difference between a quote you can trust and a guess.

Then ask for a fixed quote in CAD, before tax, with the monthly running costs listed separately. Ask what is included: product loading, payment setup, tax setup, shipping setup, basic SEO, and training on how to add products yourself. Ask what happens after launch: who fixes a bug, who updates the platform, and what that costs. Get it in writing.

If a quote seems too low, check what is missing. The most common omissions are product loading, tax configuration, and photography. A quote that includes those is usually the honest one.

For a realistic starting point, a small Canadian business selling under 50 products through Shopify should plan on $5,000 to $10,000 for the build and $100 to $300 a month to run it, plus card fees of about 3% of sales. That is the budget that covers a proper store rather than a template with your logo pasted on it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an ecommerce website cost in Canada for a small business?

Most small business ecommerce websites in Canada cost $5,000 to $25,000 CAD to build, depending on the number of products and how much is custom. A do-it-yourself Shopify store can be set up for under $1,000 plus your time, while a large custom store can run past $60,000. Monthly running costs are typically $100 to $500 plus card fees of about 3% of sales.

Is Shopify or WooCommerce cheaper in Canada?

Shopify costs $49 a month on Basic (or $37 a month billed yearly) plus card fees, with hosting and security included. WooCommerce has no platform fee but you pay separately for hosting, security, backups, and maintenance, which usually adds up to a similar monthly total. Shopify is usually cheaper in time and simpler to run; WooCommerce can be cheaper in cash if you already manage WordPress yourself.

How much does Shopify charge per sale in Canada?

Through Shopify Payments, online card payments cost 2.8% plus 30 cents per order on Basic, 2.6% plus 30 cents on Grow, and 2.4% plus 30 cents on Advanced. On a $100 online sale on Basic, that is $3.10 in fees. Using a different payment provider adds a further 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, or 0.6% on Advanced.

Do I need to charge HST on my online store in Canada?

Yes, if you are registered for GST/HST, you must charge the correct tax for the customer's province: 5% GST in Alberta, 13% HST in Ontario, GST plus QST in Quebec, and so on. Shopify Tax can calculate this automatically, but confirm your registration and settings with your accountant before launch, because the rules depend on where you are registered and where your customers are.
