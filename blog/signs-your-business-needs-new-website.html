7 signs your business needs a new website

Most business owners know their website is outdated long before they act on it. Here are seven concrete signs - and what each one is costing you.

Every business owner we talk to has the same thought: "I know our website needs work, but we're getting by." Getting by has a cost. Your website is often the first interaction a potential customer has with your business, and first impressions form in seconds.

1. It looks broken or ancient on phones

Pull up your website on your own phone right now. If the text is tiny, buttons are hard to tap, images overlap, or you have to pinch and zoom to read anything, you are losing people. More than half of website visits in Canada happen on mobile, and for local searches like "plumber near me" that share is even higher.

What it costs you: A visitor who bounces from your broken mobile site doesn't call to complain. They call your competitor instead.

Next step: Test your site on two or three different phones. If it fails, any redesign you commission must be mobile-first, not desktop shrunk down. Ask your designer to show you the mobile version first.

2. It loads slowly

Count the seconds it takes for your homepage to fully appear on a phone using mobile data, not your office wifi. If it's more than three or four seconds, that's a problem. People don't wait. Google also uses page speed as a ranking factor, so a slow site gets found less and keeps fewer of the visitors it does get.

What it costs you: Every extra second of load time drops conversions. Slow sites also quietly slide down Google results, so your competitors show up first while you fade.

Next step: Run your site through Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool. It scores your site and tells you exactly what's slow - usually oversized images or outdated plugins.

3. It never brings you enquiries

Ask yourself: when was the last time someone said "I found you through your website"? If every lead comes from word of mouth and repeat business, your website is a digital brochure that nobody reads. A business website has one job: turn visitors into enquiries.

What it costs you: Word of mouth doesn't scale. Every month your site sits there without a clear contact path, you're leaving growth entirely to chance.

Next step: Look at your site with fresh eyes. Can a stranger figure out what you do within five seconds? Is there an obvious "call now" or "get a quote" button on every page? If not, you don't need more traffic - you need a clearer path for the traffic you already have.

4. Competitors look more trustworthy online

Search your service and your city on Google and compare your listing with the businesses around you. If their sites look modern, show real photos, and list reviews - while yours looks like it hasn't changed since 2016 - customers notice. They rarely pick the business that looks like it's struggling.

What it costs you: Trust is the currency of local business. When a customer compares three options online, the outdated site gets eliminated before anyone makes a call.

Next step: You don't need to outspend competitors. You need a site that looks current, shows real photos of your work, and includes honest customer feedback.

5. You can't update it yourself

Your prices changed six months ago but the website still shows the old ones. You want to add a new service or post holiday hours, but every tiny change means emailing a developer, waiting, and paying. So nothing gets updated, and the site slowly goes stale.

What it costs you: Outdated information erodes trust. You also pay for changes in small drips that add up over a year.

Next step: Any modern website should let you edit text, photos, and prices yourself without touching code. When getting quotes, specifically ask: "Can I update the site myself, and will you show me how?" If the answer is no, keep looking.

6. Customers can't find you on Google

Search your business name on Google. Do you show up with a proper listing - hours, address, phone number, reviews? Now search what you actually do, like your trade plus your city. If you're invisible there, you're relying entirely on people who already know your name.

What it costs you: Most customers start with a Google search, not a recommendation. If you don't appear for what you sell in the city you serve, those customers go to whoever does appear.

Next step: Claim and complete your free Google Business Profile today - it takes an hour and it's the single highest-impact thing a local business can do online. Then make sure your website mentions your services and your city in plain language, the way customers actually search.

7. It doesn't reflect what your business has become

Businesses evolve. You added services, dropped others, hired people, changed locations - but the website still tells the old story. Old team photos, services you stopped offering years ago, a tagline from when you started out. Customers arrive with wrong expectations.

What it costs you: A website that undersells your current business attracts the wrong customers and misses the right ones.

Next step: Read your homepage as if you've never heard of your business. Cross out everything that's no longer true. If more than a third of the page needs rewriting, a redesign will be cheaper and cleaner than patching.

What a good redesign fixes first

If you recognized yourself in three or more of these signs, don't try to fix everything at once. A good redesign tackles the highest-impact problems in this order:

  1. Mobile experience - the site must work flawlessly on phones, because that's where most visitors are.
  2. Speed - fast loading on real mobile connections, not just office wifi.
  3. Contact path - an obvious way to reach you on every page, with as little friction as possible.
  4. Clarity - a stranger should understand what you do, who it's for, and why they should pick you, within seconds.
