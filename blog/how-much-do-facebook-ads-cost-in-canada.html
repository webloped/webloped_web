How much do Facebook ads cost in Canada in 2026?

Real numbers in CAD: what Canadian small businesses actually spend per month on Meta ads, what clicks and leads cost, what agencies charge to run it, and the math that tells you whether it is worth it.

"How much do Facebook ads cost?" is asked constantly by Canadian business owners, and the honest answer is that Facebook does not set your price. There is no rate card. You set your own budget, and what you actually pay is decided by an auction: how many other advertisers want the same audience at the same moment, and how well your ads perform against theirs.

Note: Facebook ads are bought through Meta Ads Manager, the same system that runs Instagram ads, so when this guide says "Facebook ads," it means the whole Meta platform unless stated otherwise. Everything below is framed for Canada in 2026, in Canadian dollars, with figures drawn from published 2026 benchmarks and Canadian agency guides.

The short answer

Most Canadian small businesses spend $300 to $1,500 CAD per month on Facebook and Instagram ads, with roughly $1,000 a month being the most common figure. A few hundred a month buys a real test; $2,000 to $5,000 a month is where consistent lead generation happens for competitive local markets. Add a management fee of roughly $500 to $2,000+ per month if someone runs the campaigns for you.

Canadian agencies broadly agree on a practical floor: around $1,000 CAD per month in ad spend before the platform has enough data to optimize properly. Below that, you can still run ads, but expect noisy, inconsistent results. These are planning ranges from published benchmarks and agency guides, not quotes - your actual costs depend on your industry, your city, and how good your ads are.

How Facebook ads pricing actually works

Every time there is an opening to show an ad, Meta runs an instant auction among the advertisers targeting that person. The winner is not simply the highest bidder. Meta weighs your bid together with your ad's estimated action rate (how likely the person is to engage) and ad quality. An ad people actually click and engage with can beat a higher bid from a boring ad - relevance is a discount, the same way it is on Google Ads.

You will hear three ways of talking about the price, and it helps to keep them straight:

  • CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions). What you pay for a thousand views of your ad. This is the auction's base currency; even "per click" billing is really just an outcome priced from impressions.
  • CPC (cost per click). What you pay per click through to your site. More familiar, but it is derived from the CPM and your click-through rate.
  • CPL / CPA (cost per lead, cost per acquisition). The number that actually matters: what you pay for each form fill, booking, or sale. Two businesses can have the same CPC and wildly different CPLs, because the one with the better landing page converts more of its clicks.

The two parts of your bill

Split the total cost into two separate lines, because they are often bundled in a way that hides what you are really paying:

  • Ad spend. Money paid directly to Meta for impressions and clicks. This should flow from your own Meta ad account to Meta. Ask anyone who runs ads for you to keep this separate from their fee in writing.
  • Management fee. What you pay a freelancer or agency to build, run, and optimize the campaigns: audience research, ad creative, conversion tracking, testing, and reporting.

If someone quotes you one number and you cannot tell how much goes to Meta and how much stays with them, that is a red flag. You should always know your actual ad spend.

What small businesses typically spend, by goal

Costs make more sense when grouped by what you are buying, because the same budget buys very different amounts of each:

  • Awareness and local reach ($200 to $500/month). Simple reach campaigns to a local area, or boosted posts to warm up your brand in your city. Cheap per impression, but nobody is asking for a quote yet.
  • Website traffic ($300 to $1,000/month). Click campaigns sending people to your site. Published Canadian benchmarks put Facebook and Instagram CPCs at roughly $0.50 to $3.50 per click, with 2026 industry benchmark tables reporting Canada around $0.95 per click (USD figures from US-sourced benchmark data; Canadian accounts are billed in CAD at normal conversion).
  • Lead generation ($1,000 to $3,000/month). Campaigns optimized for form fills, quote requests, and bookings. Canadian agency guides put Facebook and Instagram leads at roughly $10 to $60 per lead, depending heavily on the industry and how the lead is captured.
  • E-commerce and retargeting ($1,500 to $5,000+/month). Catalog sales, abandoned-cart retargeting, and lookalike campaigns for online stores. Retargeting warm audiences is usually the cheapest traffic you will ever buy, because the audience already knows you.

A note on USD versus CAD: most global benchmark tables report in USD. Canadian ad accounts can be billed in CAD, so when you compare your numbers to a benchmark, convert. A $14.03 USD CPM in a benchmark table is about $19 CAD - that is not the platform charging Canadians more, it is the same auction in a different currency.

Why the practical minimum is about $1,000 a month

Meta technically lets you spend $5 a day. The practical minimum is higher, and it exists for one reason: the learning phase. Meta's optimization needs roughly 50 conversions per ad set per week before it can reliably find more people like your buyers. Until the algorithm has that signal, it is guessing, and your costs bounce around.

Work the math backwards and you get the floor. If a lead costs you $20, then 50 conversions a week needs about $1,000 a week, or $4,000 a month. If leads cost $5 each in a cheap niche, the floor drops. Most Canadian small businesses land somewhere in the middle, which is why agencies keep recommending $1,000 to $1,500 a month as a starting test budget: enough to run one or two ad sets through the learning phase without spreading the budget so thin that nothing ever learns. This is also why 2026 benchmarks suggest a proper test budget of roughly $1,500 to $2,800 a month rather than a few hundred split across five campaigns.

What pushes your costs up or down

  • Audience competition. The biggest lever. Ten mortgage brokers targeting the same GTA audience costs more than one landscaper targeting a single suburb.
  • Creative quality. On Meta, the ad itself is the targeting. A thumb-stopping video or honest customer-style creative can cut your cost per result dramatically, because engagement wins auctions. Many small businesses see simple, authentic video outperform polished brand creative.
  • Audience size. Targeting too narrowly means fewer auctions to win and higher prices. Broad targeting gives the algorithm room to find the cheapest conversions inside your audience.
  • Season. Q4 is the most expensive quarter on the platform: industry data shows CPMs running about 15% higher than Q3 as every retailer floods the auction. If your business can spend in Q1 instead, the same budget buys noticeably more.
  • Geography. The GTA, Vancouver, and Montreal cost more than smaller cities, because more advertisers compete for the same people.
  • Landing page conversion rate. If your page converts 10% of visitors and a competitor's converts 5%, your cost per lead is half of theirs at the same CPC. The ad gets the click; the page closes the deal.

That last point is where most small business ad budgets quietly fail. Sending paid traffic to a slow, confusing website is the most common way to burn money on Meta. A focused landing page that matches the ad's promise, loads fast on a phone, and asks for one clear action usually outperforms a homepage. If your site is not built to convert, fix the site before scaling the spend. Our guide to slow websites covers the performance problems that waste the most paid traffic, and how to write website copy that converts covers the words on the page.

Facebook vs Instagram: do the costs differ?

They are bought through the same auction and often in the same campaign, but costs do differ by placement. Published 2026 benchmarks put Facebook CPCs at roughly $0.50 to $2.00 and Instagram CPCs at roughly $0.70 to $2.80, with Instagram CPMs a few dollars higher. Instagram tends to cost slightly more per click because the audience skews younger and more purchase-oriented, and more advertisers want that attention.

In practice, most advertisers let Meta's automatic placements choose where each ad appears, and the system shifts spend toward whichever placement is cheapest for the result they want. If Instagram Reels is converting well this week, your budget flows there. Manually locking your ads to one placement is usually worse than letting the auction sort it out, unless you have brand-safety reasons.

What agencies charge to run Meta ads in Canada

In Canada in 2026, Meta ads management typically costs $750 to $2,000+ per month, excluding ad spend. Simpler local campaigns may be managed for around $500 to $1,000 a month, while larger or more competitive accounts run $2,000 to $5,000+.

Fee structures you will see:

  • Flat monthly fee. The most common model for small business accounts. Predictable for you, and fairer than a percentage at small budgets.
  • Percentage of ad spend (10 to 20%). More common on larger accounts. Same conflict of interest as with Google Ads: it rewards the agency when you spend more, not when you spend efficiently.
  • Setup fee. Many agencies charge $500 to $2,000+ one-time for the initial build: pixel and conversion tracking, campaign structure, first round of creative, and audience research.
  • Creative as a separate line. Unlike Google search ads, Meta is a visual platform, and ad creative makes or breaks results. Some agencies quote management separately from ongoing creative production (fresh videos, images, copy). Ask who makes the ads and how often they get refreshed, because stale creative fatigues and costs climb.

What you should get for the fee: your ad spend billed separately to Meta, conversion tracking that actually works, a testing plan for creative, and reporting tied to leads or revenue rather than vanity metrics like reach and likes.

Boosted posts vs real campaigns: do not confuse them

Boosting a post from your Facebook page is the easiest way to spend money on Meta, and the least effective way to get results. Boosted posts optimize for engagement - likes, comments, shares - not for leads or sales, and you get almost none of the real targeting, testing, and tracking tools.

The honest breakdown:

  • Boosted posts are fine for getting a specific announcement in front of your existing followers or your neighbourhood. Expect to spend $100 to $300 for local visibility.
  • Ads Manager campaigns are for actual business results: leads, bookings, sales. This is where budgets of $500+ per month belong, with proper conversion tracking and landing pages.

If you have been boosting posts for months and calling it advertising, you have not really tested Meta ads yet. The jump from boosting to real campaigns is the single biggest unlock for most small businesses.

Facebook ads vs Google Ads: which should you pay for first?

They reach people in opposite mindsets. Google Ads captures existing demand: someone searches "plumber near me" and your ad appears. Meta ads create demand: someone scrolling Instagram sees your kitchen renovation video and starts a project they were not actively shopping for. One answers searches; the other interrupts scrolling.

For most Canadian small businesses, the choice looks like this:

  • Choose Google Ads first when people already search for what you sell: emergency trades, legal services, dental work. Capturing existing demand converts faster. Our Google Ads cost guide covers what that channel costs in Canada.
  • Choose Meta ads first when your product is visual, impulse-driven, or hard to search for: restaurants, salons, fitness studios, home decor, events. If a photo or video sells it better than a keyword ever could, Meta is your channel.
  • Run both together when you can: Meta retargeting is one of the cheapest ways to recover people who clicked your Google ad but did not convert. Someone who searched, visited, and left is your warmest audience - showing them a follow-up ad on Instagram is how most businesses close the loop.

Meta also tends to be cheaper per impression and click than Google search, but the traffic converts at a lower rate because the person was not shopping. Compare cost per customer, not cost per click, when you judge the two.

How to make sure the money actually works

Most wasted Meta spend is not bad bidding. It is broken fundamentals. Before you scale a budget, check these:

  1. Conversion tracking works. The Meta pixel (or Conversions API) must record form submissions, calls, and purchases accurately. Without it, the algorithm cannot learn and nobody can optimize - not you, not an agency. This is the single most common failure in small business Meta accounts.
  2. Traffic goes to focused landing pages. One page per campaign, matching the ad's promise, with one clear call to action above the fold. Your homepage is almost never the right landing page.
  3. Your site works on a phone. The vast majority of Meta traffic arrives on mobile. If your page loads slowly or the form is painful on a phone, those clicks are wasted. Run your key pages through a speed check and fix what fails.
  4. You refresh creative. Ad fatigue is real: the same creative shown to the same audience gets more expensive over time as people stop engaging. Plan to introduce fresh creative every few weeks, not every few months.
  5. You know your numbers. What is a customer worth to you over a year? What close rate does your sales process deliver? Without these, nobody can tell you whether a $30 lead is cheap or expensive. The math that matters is customer value versus cost to acquire them. If the value is higher, the channel is profitable.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a minimum budget for Facebook ads?

Meta sets no official minimum, but a practical one exists: most Canadian small businesses need around $1,000 CAD per month for campaigns to gather enough data to optimize. Below that, the algorithm rarely exits its learning phase, so results stay inconsistent, especially in competitive markets.

Do I pay Meta in Canadian dollars?

Yes, Canadian ad accounts can be billed in CAD. Just remember that most published benchmark figures are in USD from global data, so convert before comparing your costs to industry averages - a $14 USD CPM is roughly $19 CAD, which reflects the exchange rate, not a Canadian surcharge.

Are Facebook ads worth it for a small business?

They are worth it when the math works: if a customer is worth more to you than your cost to acquire them, the channel is profitable. Meta tends to suit visual, impulse, or discovery-driven businesses best. The businesses that struggle usually have broken conversion tracking, stale creative, or budgets too small for the learning phase - all fixable problems.

Should I run ads myself or hire someone?

DIY works if you are willing to learn Ads Manager, get conversion tracking right first, and spend a few hours a month testing creative. The most common DIY mistake is confusing boosted posts with real campaigns. An agency or freelancer is worth the fee when your budget is significant, your market is competitive, or your time is better spent running the business. Between the two, a few hours with an ads consultant to set up tracking and campaign structure can give a DIY account a solid start for a fraction of a retainer.
