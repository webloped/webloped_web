How to add online booking to your website

Most appointment enquiries arrive outside business hours. Here is how to let customers book directly from your website, step by step, with no coding required.

If you run a service business - a clinic, salon, law office, trades company, tutoring service, or consultancy - a large share of your customers decide to book outside your business hours. Someone with a toothache finds your site at 11 PM, or a homeowner finally gets around to calling a contractor on Sunday morning. If all your website offers is a phone number and a contact form, many of those people move on to the competitor whose site lets them book right then and there.

Adding online booking to your website means customers can see your availability, pick a time, and get a confirmation without you lifting a finger. The good news is that you do not need a custom-built system. Modern booking tools handle the scheduling logic, and most of them drop into an existing website with a copy-and-paste embed code. This guide walks through the whole process the way we set it up for clients.

Step 1: Choose a booking tool

This is the one decision that matters most, because switching tools later means rebuilding your settings. The right choice depends on how you work, so start by answering three questions: how many services do you offer, do you take payment at booking time, and do you need it to work in French or English (or both)?

For most solo and small Canadian service businesses, these are the reliable options:

  • Calendly. Best for consultations and one-on-one meetings. Very easy to set up, with a free tier that covers basic scheduling. Works well for accountants, consultants, coaches, and agencies.
  • Square Appointments. Strong if you already use Square for payments in person. Includes free booking features on some plans and gives you an embed code or button for your existing website.
  • Acuity Scheduling (by Squarespace). Good for salons, wellness businesses, and anyone who needs deposits, packages, or intake forms. Heavier feature set, at a price to match.
  • Fresha. Built for beauty and wellness, with a marketplace that can also send you new clients. Popular with barbershops and nail salons in Canada.

One Canadian-specific check: make sure your chosen tool handles Canadian time zones correctly (some default to US time zones) and that its payment integration works with Canadian banks and cards if you plan to take deposits. Also confirm that customer data is stored with reasonable privacy protections, since PIPEDA applies to your business regardless of which tool you pick.

Honest note: avoid signing a long annual contract with a tool you have not tested. Every tool above offers a free tier or trial. Run a full week of real bookings through it first. A tool that is painful for you to manage will quietly kill the whole project, because you will stop keeping your availability up to date.

Step 2: Set up your services, durations, and availability

Before touching your website, get the scheduling logic right inside the tool. This is the part people rush, and it is where most booking setups go wrong.

  • List each service with its real duration. Add buffer time between appointments. If a haircut takes 45 minutes but you need 15 minutes to reset the station, set the service to 60 minutes. Over-optimistic durations are the number one cause of a booking system that looks broken within a month.
  • Set your real availability, not your office hours. Mark personal blocks, lunch, and days off. If you do house calls on Fridays and are only in the office Monday to Thursday, reflect that.
  • Add limits. Set how far ahead people can book and how late they can cancel or reschedule. A 48-hour cancellation window is common for service businesses, and 24 hours is normal for lower-cost appointments.
  • Write a cancellation policy in plain language. Customers read this when they book, so state what happens if they do not show up or cancel late. Decide this before you launch, not after the first no-show makes you angry.

Step 3: Add it to your website

There are two ways to put booking on your site, and the right one depends on your setup:

  • Embed it on a dedicated booking page. Almost every tool gives you a snippet of code to paste into your site so the calendar appears right on the page. This keeps visitors on your website, which is the better option for most businesses. Your web person can usually do this in under an hour, and most website builders have a "custom code" block that accepts it.
  • Link to a hosted booking page. Every tool also gives you a booking URL, something like yourbusiness.booking-tool.com. You can simply link your "Book Now" buttons to it. Slightly less seamless, but it works immediately with zero technical setup.

Whichever you choose, put a "Book Now" button in your main navigation and in the hero section of your homepage. Booking buried on a contact page might as well not exist. Add it to your service pages too: someone reading about your physiotherapy services is one click away from booking an assessment, and that click should be right there on the page.

Also add your booking link to your Google Business Profile. Google shows a booking button directly on your listing for many service categories, and that listing is where a lot of your discovery happens.

Honest note: test the whole flow on a phone before you announce it. Most bookings happen on mobile, and an embedded calendar that is cramped or hard to tap on a small screen will silently cost you appointments. Book a fake appointment for yourself and walk through every screen.

Step 4: Decide on payments and deposits

You do not have to take payment at booking time, but for higher-value appointments it is worth considering. A small deposit, or simply a card on file, dramatically reduces no-shows because the customer has something at stake.

If you do take deposits, connect the booking tool to a payment processor that works in Canada, such as Stripe or Square, both of which support CAD and Canadian bank payouts. Keep the deposit reasonable, usually 20 to 50 percent of the service price or a fixed amount for free consultations. And make sure your cancellation policy states clearly when a deposit is refundable, because disputes over deposits are a common source of chargebacks.

If you do not need payment upfront, skip it. Every extra step at booking time lowers completion rates. A simple booking with a confirmation is better than a complex booking people abandon halfway.

Step 5: Turn on reminders to cut no-shows

No-shows are the quietest way a service business loses money. A missed appointment slot cannot be rebooked at the last minute, and the hour is just gone. Every serious booking tool can send automatic reminders, usually a confirmation immediately and a reminder the day before, each with a simple way to confirm, cancel, or reschedule.

Turn these on from day one. They do not eliminate no-shows, but they cut them noticeably because most no-shows are caused by people simply forgetting, not by people deliberately skipping.

One Canadian compliance note: automated texts and emails about appointments are generally fine under CASL when there is an existing business relationship or the message is about a booked appointment, but marketing messages require consent. Keep your reminder messages transactional, and do not slip promotional content into them unless the customer opted in separately.

Step 6: Tell your customers it exists

A booking button that nobody knows about is decoration. Once the system works, put the word out: mention it in your email signature, your Instagram bio link, your Google Business Profile, and on any printed material you already hand out. If you take bookings by phone, have your staff mention the online option at the end of every call. After a few weeks, the phone calls start shifting to online bookings on their own.

Check in after the first month. Look at how many bookings came through, how many no-shows you had, and whether your availability settings need adjusting. A booking system is not set-and-forget in the first month, but once the settings match your real schedule, it largely runs itself.

What does it cost?

For most small businesses, the booking tool itself runs from free to roughly the cost of a phone plan per month in CAD, depending on the tool and tier. The free tiers of the main tools cover basic one-person scheduling, and you generally only need a paid plan once you add multiple staff members, deposits, or integrations. Check current pricing on the tool's website before committing, since pricing and tiers change.

Getting it onto your website is a small one-time job: adding a "Book Now" page, placing buttons through the site, and testing the mobile flow. If you are already having a website built or updated, ask for booking to be included in the scope rather than bolted on later. You can see what this typically involves on our pricing page.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add online booking to my existing website, or do I need a new one?

You can add it to almost any existing website. Booking tools are designed to work with any site through an embed code or a link to a hosted booking page. You only need a new website if your current one is already failing you in other ways, like not working on phones or being painfully slow.

How do customers pay when they book online?

If you choose to take payment at booking time, the tool connects to a processor like Stripe or Square, and customers pay by card in CAD. You can take a full payment, a deposit, or just hold card details for no-show protection. If you do not need upfront payment, customers simply book and pay you later as usual.

Will online booking work for a business with multiple staff?

Yes. All the major tools let customers pick a specific team member or let you route bookings by service. You set each person's hours and services, and the tool only shows slots where the right person is available. This is exactly what salons, clinics, and trades companies with field staff use.

What happens if a customer needs to cancel or reschedule?

The confirmation and reminder messages include a link the customer uses to manage their own appointment, within the cancellation window you set. You get notified automatically. This alone removes a surprising amount of phone tag from your week.

Online booking is one of the highest-return additions a service business can make to its website. It captures the customers who are ready to buy when you are not at your desk, and it removes the back-and-forth that eats your admin time. If your business runs on appointments and your website still only offers a phone number, this is the gap worth closing.
