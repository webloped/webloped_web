How to get your business on Google: a practical guide

The honest answer up front: getting your business on Google comes down to four things - a complete Google Business Profile, a website Google can read, consistent information everywhere, and real customer reviews. None of it costs anything, and all of it is doable without an agency. Here is how, step by step.

When someone in your town searches "plumber near me" or "best bakery in Cambridge," Google decides which businesses to show. That decision is not random, and it is not paid placement - the listings Google shows for free are earned. Every week we talk to business owners who are invisible in those results even though they have been trading for years. Almost always, the fix is the same short list of fundamentals that were never done properly, not some secret technique.

This guide walks through those fundamentals in order of impact. It is written for Canadian small businesses - a tradesperson in Hamilton, a salon in Mississauga, a bakery in Kitchener - and everything in it is something you can do yourself, this week, at no cost.

Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the single highest-impact thing you can do, so do it first. Your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the listing Google shows in the map results and in the local pack - those three businesses with a map that appear above the regular results on local searches. If you do nothing else from this guide, do this.

  • Claim it. Go to business.google.com, search for your business, and claim it. If it does not exist, create it. Google will verify you own it, usually by mailing a postcard to your address or calling your phone.
  • Complete every field. Hours, phone number, website, business description, service areas, accessibility details - all of it. An incomplete profile loses to a complete one for the same search, so do not leave blanks you could fill.
  • Pick the right category. Choose the most specific primary category that matches your main service ("Plumber," not "Home Service"). Add secondary categories for the rest of what you do. Categories directly affect which searches your listing appears for, so get them right.
  • Write a real description. You get a few hundred characters - use them to say what you do, who you serve, and where. Use the words your customers actually search for, naturally, not stuffed in as a keyword list.
  • Add real photos. At least ten: your storefront or work vehicle, your team at work, finished jobs, your products. Real photos beat stock photos completely - Google and customers can both tell the difference.

Then treat it as a living thing, not a one-time setup. Post an update or a photo once a month, keep your holiday hours current, and check the stats Google gives you - calls, direction requests, website clicks - so you can see what is working.

Step 2: Build a website Google can read

A Business Profile can get you into the map results, but Google trusts businesses more when the listing is backed by a real website. It also gives Google more to rank you for: your profile covers a description, but your website can cover every service, every suburb, and every question a customer might ask. If you already have a site, check it against this list. If you do not, our guide on how much a website costs in Canada helps you scope the investment realistically.

  • Give each core service its own page. A plumber should have separate pages for drain cleaning, water heaters, and emergency calls - not one page that lists everything. Each page is a chance to rank for a different search.
  • Use the words customers search for. Write service pages in the plain language a customer would type into Google, not industry jargon. Your heading structure matters: one clear H1 per page that matches the service and location.
  • Name your city naturally in the text. "We serve Cambridge, Kitchener, and Waterloo" in a sentence beats a keyword-stuffed footer list. Your service area should appear in prose on your homepage and contact page.
  • Make it fast and mobile-friendly. Most local searches happen on phones. If your site takes more than a few seconds to load, or the phone number is not tappable to call, you are losing the exact customers who found you.
  • Have a real contact page. Your full address and phone number, displayed as text Google can read - not just an image, and not just a form.

One detail that trips people up: your name, address, and phone number must be identical everywhere they appear - your website, your Google profile, and every directory. "St." in one place and "Street" in another looks like two different businesses to Google. Pick one version and use it everywhere.

Step 3: Get reviews, and answer every one

Reviews do double duty. Customers read them before choosing - most people check reviews before contacting a local business - and Google reads them as evidence that you are real, active, and good at what you do. Recent reviews signal that you are still in business and still delivering; a profile whose last review is two years old looks dormant.

  • Ask at the right moment. Right after the job is done, when the customer is happiest. Send them a one-tap link to your Google review page - the longer the gap, the fewer reviews you get.
  • Make it a habit, not a campaign. One or two reviews a week for a year beats thirty reviews in one weekend and then silence. Steady beats spiky.
  • Reply to every review. Thank the happy ones, and handle the unhappy ones calmly and publicly. How you respond to a bad review tells future customers more about your business than the bad review itself does.
  • Never buy reviews. Google removes fake reviews and can penalize the listing. It is not worth it, and real customers are usually willing to leave one if you ask.

This is the part most owners put off because it feels awkward. Make it a line in your process - "we will send you a quick link, a review takes sixty seconds" - and the awkwardness goes away within a week.

Step 4: Get listed in the places Google checks

Google cross-references your business details against other sites - directories, review platforms, industry listings. These are called citations, and consistent listings across them reinforce that your business is legitimate and that your address and phone number are correct.

  • Start with the free essentials. In Canada, that means Bing Places, Apple Maps, YellowPages.ca, 411.ca, and Yelp, plus your industry's main directory (HomeStars for trades, for example). Claim or correct each one.
  • Match your details exactly. Same business name, same address format, same phone number, same hours. Consistency is the whole point.
  • Skip the mass-submission services. Being on a hundred obscure directories adds nothing; being correct on the dozen real ones matters. Quality over quantity.

Step 5: Be patient and keep going

This is the step nobody wants to read, so here it is plainly: local SEO takes time. Your Business Profile can start appearing in local results within days or weeks of verification. Actually ranking well - in the local pack, for your main searches - typically takes two to four months of the fundamentals being in place, with results compounding over six to twelve months as reviews accumulate and Google's confidence in your data grows.

Businesses in smaller towns often move faster, because there is less competition. In a dense market like the GTA, expect the full stretch. The owners who win are not the ones who did one intense week of optimization. They are the ones whose profile quietly gained four photos, six reviews, and a fresh post every month. Boring wins.

That is also why this is worth doing yourself rather than paying a monthly retainer for "SEO" in the early days. The work is consistency: complete the profile, fix the website, build the review habit, keep the listings correct. If you do that for a year, you will outperform most of your competitors simply because most of them never finished step one.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get my business on Google?

A Google Business Profile can start appearing in local searches within days to weeks of verification. Ranking well takes longer: a completed profile plus steady reviews typically shows movement in two to four months, and results keep compounding over six to twelve months. Businesses in smaller towns often move faster than businesses in dense city markets.

Is it free to get my business on Google?

Yes. Claiming and maintaining a Google Business Profile is free, and collecting reviews is free. The costs that sometimes appear are the time it takes to do the work properly, and - if you hire help - what a freelancer or agency charges to manage your profile, website, and reviews for you.

Can I get my business on Google without a website?

You can appear in Google Maps and local results with just a complete Google Business Profile, and many service businesses start exactly that way. But a website gives Google far more to work with: your services in detail, your service area in prose, and proof of legitimacy. Businesses that rank well long-term almost always have both.

What is the Google local pack, and why does it matter?

The local pack is the block of three business listings with a map that Google shows at the top of local searches like "plumber near me" or "dentist in Cambridge." It appears above the regular web results, which is why appearing in it drives far more calls and clicks than a regular organic listing. A complete Google Business Profile with reviews is the main way into it.
